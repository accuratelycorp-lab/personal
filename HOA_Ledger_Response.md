## 2. Why they may have done it (and their likely argument)
- Portals like AppFolio generally apply a generic payment to the **oldest open charge first**. The fine (charged earlier) was older than October dues.
- Declaration §3.3 calls fines an "individual assessment" and §3.4 lets the lien secure fines. They will say the fine was just the oldest delinquent item. Bylaws §6.7 also says the Association is "not bound by any restrictive endorsement, designation, or instruction" on a payment.
- **Your counters:** (a) the fine is disputed and you've asked for a hold, so a dues payment shouldn't be swept into it; (b) the fine's validity is in question (7-day notice vs 14-day requirement); (c) the sequence in Bylaws §6.7 (interest → late fee → costs → "oldest delinquent assessment") doesn't mention fines, and Florida's payment-order rule for assessments (§720.3085(3)) also lists only interest, late fees, costs/attorney fees, then assessments [VERIFY]; (d) the late charges exist only because of the allocation; (e) they were on notice of the dispute (your 10/5 email) before charging more.
