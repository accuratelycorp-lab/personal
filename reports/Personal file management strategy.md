# Replace family folder trees with metadata and a Mac mini hub

**Bottom line: stop hand-building per-family and per-event folder trees, and move that information into file metadata where machines can apply it.** The physical layer should be one machine-generated chronological tree on the QNAP (`Archive/Photos/YYYY/YYYY-MM-DD_slug/`), and every other dimension (own family, wife's family, mother's side, father's side, birthdays, projects) should be a keyword or person name written into the files' XMP. The Mac mini becomes the always-on junction between all three clouds and the NAS, because it is the only device in the household that can run Zoho TrueSync, Google Drive for desktop and iCloud Drive at the same time; Hazel 6.1 (**$42 one-time**) on the mini routes a single inbox into WorkDrive (business), Google Drive (personal dump) and the NAS archive. Local AI earns its place as a *tagger*, not a *mover*: Apple Photos' free on-device face recognition, exported nightly by osxphotos as person keywords, is the only reliable identity signal for the family-branch split; a local vision model (Qwen3-VL 8B via Ollama) adds scene/object keywords; Paperless-ngx 3.x with Ollama classifies documents on the NAS. Deterministic rules keyed on those keywords do all the moving. The total new spend is roughly **$42–$110 one-time** (Hazel, plus optionally PhotoSync Pro and ChronoSync or Carbon Copy Cloner) with zero new subscriptions, and the ongoing manual work shrinks to a 15-minute weekly triage of one inbox. The honest limits: iOS will never run a third-party uploader reliably in the background, Zoho WorkDrive has no WebDAV and no QNAP connector so it is reachable only through the Mac, Apple's face *regions* and clusters never leave Photos (only names do), and no vision model can tell your wife's cousin from your father's.

## One date tree plus keywords beats four family trees

The per-family-branch structure fails for a structural reason, not a discipline reason: a folder is one-to-one, and a Christmas photo with both sides of the family present can only live in one place, so either it is duplicated or one branch's folder is silently incomplete. Lightroom Classic's organising model makes the division of labour explicit: folders mirror the physical disk location and should handle "permanent storage organization, backup management, chronological filing"; collections are virtual, so one photo can sit in dozens of them while existing in one folder; keywords are searchable metadata that travel with exported files ([Markus Hagner Photography](https://markus-hagner-photography.com/how-to-organize-photos-using-folders-collections-and-keywords-in-lightroom-classic/)). The practitioner compromise that keeps event recall without event folders is a dated folder with a short slug, such as **"2024-11-16 Lovely Lake birds"**, so alphabetical order equals chronological order and the event is still readable ([BCG Forums](https://bcgforums.com/threads/how-do-you-organize-your-collections-in-lightroom-classic.40376)). Where tag-based systems do fail, the forum evidence shows it is vocabulary sprawl: users report keywords that "kept adding complexity" and older folders that "weren't updated with the newer keywords" ([Talk Photography](https://www.talkphotography.co.uk/threads/folders-in-lightroom.638165/)). The fix is a controlled vocabulary of a dozen terms (four family-branch terms, a handful of event types, the children's and grandparents' names) applied at import, not a richer taxonomy.

The migration from the existing trees is mechanical and loses nothing, because the folder names *are* the keywords. ExifTool's author gives the one-liner: `exiftool "-subject<directory" -sep "/" -r DIR` writes each path component as a separate `XMP:Subject` keyword, with variants that strip a leading date and keep only the last folder, and `-api nodups` so reruns are safe ([ExifTool forum](https://exiftool.org/forum/index.php?msg=49360); [ExifTool forum](https://exiftool.org/forum/index.php?msg=85824)). Phil Harvey recommends writing `XMP:Subject` rather than legacy `IPTC:Keywords` ([ExifTool forum](https://exiftool.org/forum/index.php?msg=49360)). After that pass, "Wife's family/Birthdays/2019" becomes keywords "Wife's family", "Birthdays" and "2019" embedded in every JPEG and HEIC, and the files can be re-filed by `DateTimeOriginal` into the date tree without a single judgment call. Run an exact-duplicate pass first with czkawka (Rust, multi-threaded, finds duplicates and similar images, macOS build available) ([Linux Uprising](https://linuxuprising.com/2021/03/find-and-remove-duplicate-files-similar.html)), and use PhotoSweeper (**$14.99 one-time**, understands Apple Photos, Lightroom and Capture One libraries) for anything already inside a Photos library rather than deduplicating on the filesystem ([AppAddict](https://scour.ing/@AnnaMaria/p/https://appaddict.app/post/photosweeper-powerful-adaptable-affordable-one-time-purchase)).

Portability is the reason to standardise on flat `XMP-dc:Subject` keywords as the durable truth. Immich reads tags from `digiKam:TagsList`, `lr:HierarchicalSubject` and `IPTC:Keywords` in that priority order and requires sidecars named `IMG_0001.jpg.xmp` or `IMG_0001.xmp` ([Immich docs: XMP sidecars](https://docs.immich.app/features/xmp-sidecars)); PhotoPrism's XMP reader is self-described as a proof of concept covering a subset of fields ([PhotoPrism metadata docs](https://docs.photoprism.app/user-guide/library/metadata/)); and Apple Photos only reads people names embedded as `XMP-dc:Subject` inside the JPEG, never from a third-party sidecar ([Apple Community](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/254932358?page=1)). Face rectangles are the least portable element: the MWG Regions structure is "the most standard tag" for them ([ExifTool forum](https://exiftool.org/forum/index.php?msg=64723)), but Apple Photos' own export writes names into XMP `dc:Subject` and omits rectangles ([Lightroom Queen forum](https://www.lightroomqueen.com/community/threads/how-do-i-import-mac-face-tages-as-lrc-people-keywords.42716/)). So encode family branch as a keyword whose flat form is self-explanatory ("Jane Smith" plus "Smith side"), embed for JPEG/HEIC, and use sidecars only for RAW and video, which cannot be written into.

For video, keep project folders, but only for *edited* projects; raw iPhone clips of a birthday belong in the date tree with a keyword, not in a project folder. The cross-NLE template editors converge on is numbered (01_Footage, 02_Audio, 03_Graphics, 04_Project, 05_Exports with Drafts/Finals), built on disk before import and mirrored as bins ([davinciresolve21.com](https://davinciresolve21.com/blog/davinci-resolve-folder-and-bin-naming-conventions-for-organized-projects)). For Final Cut Pro the safe pattern is the `.fcpbundle` on local SSD and camera originals on the NAS via "Leave Files in Place", with 2.5GbE adequate for ProRes 422 and 10GbE for ProRes 4444 or high-bitrate RAW; a library on a network share is not Apple-supported ([needtoknowit.com.au](https://needtoknowit.com.au/blog/final-cut-pro-nas-setup/)). Archiving a finished project is scriptable: delete generated library files, consolidate media, then move the self-contained library to an APFS volume that is not a Time Machine disk ([Apple Support 119610](https://support.apple.com/119610)).

## The Mac mini becomes the junction for every cloud and the NAS

The constraint that shapes the whole architecture is that two of the three clouds cannot be reached from the NAS at all. QNAP's HBS 3 provider lists name Google Drive, OneDrive, Dropbox, Box, S3-class, WebDAV, FTP and rsync, and never Zoho ([QNAP 2017 HBS release](https://www.qnap.com/en-us/news/2017/qnap-officially-releases-hybrid-backup-sync-to-streamline-backup-restoration-and-synchronization)); WorkDrive has no WebDAV endpoint, only years-old unanswered feature requests ([Zoho Community](https://help.zoho.com/portal/en-gb/community/topic/webdav-ftp-sftp-protocols-for-syncing?page=5)); and rclone's lead developer calls WorkDrive "almost unusable with rclone due to rate limiting" ([rclone forum](https://forum.rclone.org/t/rclone-invokes-more-number-of-workdrives-files-listing-api-calls-which-exceeds-the-throttling-limit/43037)), with no modtime or checksum support, so it cannot detect changes reliably ([rclone zoho docs](https://git.frostfs.info/satyamisme/rclone/raw/tag/v1.66.0/docs/content/zoho.md)). iCloud Drive is similar: no NAS vendor offers a native target, and rclone's `iclouddrive` backend (v1.69, January 2025) demands your main Apple ID password with 2FA, re-authentication every 30 days, and Advanced Data Protection switched off ([rclone: iclouddrive](https://rclone.org/iclouddrive/)). The only machine that can hold all three sync clients is a Mac, and the mini is the one that can stay on.

|---|---|---|

| **Mac Studio** | Editing workstation; active FCP/Resolve projects on local NVMe, footage on NAS "Leave in place" | WorkDrive online-only; local `~/Inbox` that Hazel/Syncthing hands to the mini |
| **MacBook** | Mobile capture and office work | Same inbox pattern; never let Drive or Zoho own Desktop/Documents |
| **iPhone / iPad** | Capture; iCloud Photos is canonical; ProRes recorded direct to exFAT SSD | Optional PhotoSync Pro to NAS for same-day copies (not a backup) |
| **QNAP** | System of record: `Archive/` date tree, `Inbox/` landing share, Time Machine share, daily snapshots, HBS 3 to Google Drive, Qfiling, Container Station (Immich or QuMagie, Paperless-ngx) | HBS 3 ≥26.2.0.938; snapshots with reserved space |
| **Zoho WorkDrive** | Business working set only | Reached only via TrueSync on the mini (inbox folder "Make offline") |
| **Google Drive** | Personal dump plus encrypted off-site backup target | Drive for desktop in Mirror mode for the dump folder; HBS 3 one-way job for `Archive/` |
| **iCloud+ 200 GB** | Photo capture, device backups, `iCloud Drive/Inbox` transit | Originals live on the NAS, so iCloud never needs to grow |
| **Portable SSDs** | One exFAT for iPhone ProRes/iPad transfers; one APFS for the Photos library; one rotated cold clone | ChronoSync or CCC verified copy on mount |

The mini's job starts with photos. With "Optimize Storage" on, Apple keeps originals in iCloud and "space-saving versions" on the device; with "Download Originals" the full-resolution files are kept locally as well ([Apple Community](https://origin-discussions2-us-dr-prz.apple.com/thread/254682229)). Apple does not support a Photos library on network shares, SD cards or USB sticks; it must sit on a directly attached APFS or HFS+ drive that is not a Time Machine disk ([Apple Support HT201517, archived](https://webcf.waybackmachine.org/web/20210812034547/https://support.apple.com/en-us/HT201517)). So the mini holds the canonical Photos library on an attached APFS SSD and a nightly launchd job runs osxphotos, which exports with `--directory "{created.year}/{created.month}"` style templates, writes XMP sidecars or embeds metadata with `--exiftool`, adds every recognised person as a keyword with `--person-keyword`, exports the Live Photo MOV by default, and runs incrementally with `--update` ([osxphotos README](https://github.com/RhetTbull/osxphotos)). Its `--exiftool` path writes `XMP:Subject`, `IPTC:Keywords` and `XMP:PersonInImage`, and `--sidecar xmp` additionally emits MWG and Microsoft face regions, making osxphotos the only tool found that exports Apple face geometry at all ([osxphotos export.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/RhetTbull/osxphotos/main/osxphotos/cli/export.py)). This single job replaces every hand-built family folder: name people once in Photos, and the NAS archive receives "Jane Smith" as a keyword on every photo she appears in. Two caveats: osxphotos has had only limited testing on macOS 26 and cannot read shared albums there, and `--download-missing` relies on Photos' "somewhat buggy" AppleScript interface ([osxphotos README](https://github.com/RhetTbull/osxphotos)), which is another reason to keep "Download Originals" on so nothing is missing.

Documents follow the tenant-first rule: the top level of the tree is *who owns the data*, because WorkDrive's retention, trash and sharing are governed by a business admin console ([Zoho WorkDrive Data Retention Policy](https://help.zoho.com/portal/en/kb/workdrive/teams/admin-console/settings/articles/data-retention-policy)) while the personal clouds follow consumer rules. Inside each tenant use PARA (Projects, Areas, Resources, Archives) for working documents ([Lark](https://www.larksuite.com/es_mx/blog/para-method)) and, for reference material others must find, Johnny.Decimal's rigid two-level numbering (at most 10 areas × 10 categories × 100 IDs, "only two levels deep") ([johnnydecimal.com](https://johnnydecimal.com/documentation/the-numbers.md)). Apple is explicit that before enabling iCloud Desktop & Documents you must "turn off any other third-party app or cloud service that syncs your Desktop or Documents folder" ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-kz/guide/mac-help/mchl1a02d711/12.0/mac)), and Google's own docs say local folders like Desktop "can only be mirrored" ([Google Support](https://support.google.com/drive/answer/13401938?hl=en)); let exactly one service own those folders, or none.

Routing happens in Hazel on the mini. Hazel 6.1 costs **$42 single-user** ($65 family pack, $20 upgrade), the licence is perpetual for the major version, it requires macOS 13+, and 6.1 added folder options for cloud-only files precisely because TrueSync and Drive are File Provider folders full of placeholders ([Noodlesoft FAQ](https://www.noodlesoft.com/?p=1852); [Noodlesoft 6.1 notes](https://www.noodlesoft.com/?p=2008)). Hazel 6 can match text inside images and PDFs that have no text layer via on-the-fly OCR ([Noodlesoft](https://www.noodlesoft.com/?p=1997)) and runs shell scripts as conditions or actions with the file path in `$1` ([Noodlesoft: shell scripts](https://www.noodlesoft.com/?p=977)). TrueSync is a File Provider extension on macOS 12+, all files are online-only by default, and "when you upload files into an offline folder, those files will be made offline" ([Zoho: TrueSync for Mac](https://help.zoho.com/portal/en/kb/workdrive/desktop-apps/workdrive-truesync/articles/truesync-for-mac)), so a WorkDrive folder marked offline on the mini is a reliable upload target that Hazel can move files into. Drive for desktop on macOS 12.1+ also lives under `~/Library/CloudStorage` via File Provider ([Google Support](https://support.google.com/drive/answer/12178485?hl=en)). Note that Finder tags on Google Drive files are not propagated to other devices ([MacScripter](https://www.macscripter.net/t/duplicating-finders-file-tags-on-google-drives-files/74207)), which makes tags a strictly local routing signal: apply `biz` or `home` on the Mac, let Hazel act on it, and never expect it to survive the cloud hop. The practical rule chain is cheap first, expensive last: a `YYYY-MM-DD_<client|home>_desc` filename or a Finder tag decides most files instantly; Hazel's OCR "contents contain" catches invoices and receipts by VAT number or client name; and only the remainder goes to a shell script that asks a local model for a single word, BUSINESS or PERSONAL, which Hazel then writes back as a tag.

The headless-Mac gotcha is real: after a power loss the mini sits at the login screen and "none of the applications like Hazel... are available until logged in" ([MPU forum](https://talk.macpowerusers.com/t/hosting-hazel-rules-on-remote-server/8252)). Accept auto-login on the home LAN and buy a UPS; the alternative is remembering to log in after every outage.

Backup follows 3-2-1-1-0 (three copies, two media, one off-site, one immutable or air-gapped, zero errors on test restore) ([Zmanda](https://www.zmanda.com/blog/understanding-the-3-2-1-1-0-backup-rule/)). QNAP snapshots are block-level and "remain safe even if the file system is affected by ransomware" ([QNAP home snapshots](https://www.qnap.com/en-uk/solution/home-snapshots)); Time Machine from each Mac goes to a QNAP share flagged for Time Machine over SMB 3 ([QNAP tutorial](https://www.qnap.com/en/how-to/tutorial/con_show.php?cid=268)). HBS 3 backs up NAS folders to Google Drive with scheduling, versioning and filters ([QNAP FAQ](https://www.qnap.com/en-ca/how-to/faq/article/how-do-i-back-up-data-to-google-drive-using-hbs3-and-restore-it)); its client-side encryption "cannot be disabled, and the password cannot be changed" once applied ([QNAP HBS 3 docs](https://docs.qnap.com/application/hybrid-backup-sync/3v21.x/en-us/creating-a-backup-job-753C8301.html)), so escrow the password in the cold-SSD bag. With versioning on, HBS 3 may auto-enable QuDedup and produce `.qdff` containers instead of browsable files ([QNAP FAQ](https://www.qnap.com/en-gr/how-to/faq/article/why-does-hbs-3-apply-qudedup-deduplication-automatically-in-some-backup-jobs)), which is actually desirable for a photo archive because Google Drive throttles file creation to a few per second and caps uploads at **750 GB per rolling 24 hours** ([University of Alaska KB](https://service.alaska.edu/TDClient/36/Portal/KB/PrintArticle?ID=117)). If the Google account is the consumer AI Pro plan, storage went to **5 TB on 1 April 2026** at no extra cost ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/04/01/google-ai-pro-5-tb/)), which comfortably holds an encrypted copy of a family archive; the research could not confirm which Google edition "Google Pro" refers to, and a Workspace Business Starter account would have only 30 GB pooled ([Google Workspace Admin Help](https://pss.support.google.com/a/answer/10071955)), so check before sizing the job. iCloud Photos is sync, not backup: deleting on any device deletes everywhere, with 30 days of recovery ([Apple Community](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/254852780)). Zoho advertises unlimited file versions ([Zoho Community](https://help.zoho.com/portal/pt/community/topic/tips-tricks-14-stay-up-to-date-with-workdrives-unlimited-version-history?page=1)), but because nothing can pull from WorkDrive, the business's only copy under your own control is the mini's offline TrueSync folder captured by Time Machine. Verify the cold clone with ChronoSync (**$49.99 one-time**, "Verify Copied Data" reads every file back) ([Econ Technologies](https://www.econtechnologies.com/product/chronosync)) or Carbon Copy Cloner 7 (**$49.99** household licence) ([Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/2405539/carbon-copy-cloner-review.html)); plain rsync is the free alternative.

## Local AI tags well but must never pick the folder

The flagship open-source "AI organisers" are not daily drivers. LlamaFS uses Groq cloud inference by default, needs code edits to switch to local Ollama, and last pushed in August 2025 ([LlamaFS README](https://github.com/iyaja/llama-fs); [gittrend](https://gittrend.io/repo/iyaja/llama-fs)); Local File Organizer's last release was v0.0.2 in September 2024 on a Llama 3.2 3B plus LLaVA 1.6 stack with prompts edited in Python source ([GitHub](https://github.com/QiuYannnn/Local-File-Organizer)). Both bundle the model with the file-moving logic and both ship a dry-run step because the output is a free-text guess. The pattern that has displaced them is narrower and sounder: a local vision-language model writes keywords and captions into XMP, and a rule engine moves files based on those fields. exif-ai is a ready CLI that does exactly this through Ollama, defaulting to `llama3.2-vision`, writing tags to `Subject`, `TagsList` and `Keywords`, with `--dry-run` and a custom `--tag-prompt` ([exif-ai](https://github.com/tychenjiajun/exif-ai)). Ollama ships official Qwen3-VL in `qwen3-vl:4b` and `qwen3-vl:8b` variants ([Ollama blog](https://ollama.com/blog/qwen3-vl)), and mlx-vlm runs Qwen3-VL, Gemma 4, Florence-2 and Moondream natively on Apple Silicon with an OpenAI-compatible `mlx_vlm.server` on port 8080 ([mlx-vlm](https://github.com/Blaizzy/mlx-vlm)). Llama 3.2 Vision 11B at Q4 fits in roughly 8.5 GB on a 16 GB Mac mini ([llmconfigurator](https://www.llmconfigurator.com/pl/can-i-run/llama-3-2-vision-on-mac-mini-m4-16gb)), so the Mac Studio is the natural host for the larger models and the mini for an 8B-class one.

The honest capability boundary is identity. No source shows a VLM reliably distinguishing specific individuals, and vendor tools acknowledge the hallucination problem: Tag Your Photos deliberately keeps labels general ("bird" rather than species) when uncertain ([completeaitraining](https://completeaitraining.com/ai-tools/tag-your-photos/)). A document-classification test of Gemma 4 E4B on CPU took **25.8 seconds median per suggestion** and reasoning models ignored "no reasoning" instructions and flooded structured output with extra tokens ([jacar.es](https://jacar.es/en/paperless-ngx-3-local-ai-ollama/)). So constrain the prompt to a fixed label list returned as JSON, keep a review step, and never let the model choose a destination for people-based categories. The mapping from "Jane Smith" to "wife's family" is a lookup table in Hazel or a script, not a model output.

Identity comes from face recognition that learns named people, and the household already has the best free option running. Apple Photos recognises people on-device, and the exportable unit is the name as a keyword via osxphotos as described above. For a filesystem-first alternative, digiKam 8.6 rewrote faces around YuNet detection and SFace embeddings with a KNN+SVM ensemble ([digiKam 8.6.0](https://digikam.org/news/2025-03-15-8.6.0_release_announcement)), auto-tags with EfficientNet-B7 (~1,000 classes) or YOLOv11 ([digiKam docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KDE/digikam-doc/master/maintenance_tools/maintenance_autotags.rst)), and is the only tool found that writes MWG face regions into XMP ([KDE bug 277429](https://bugs.kde.org/show_bug.cgi?id=277429)). Among commercial one-time licences, Excire Foto 2027 (released June 2026) is **$249**, runs all AI locally, adds text-in-image recognition and people search ([PhotoWorkout](https://www.photoworkout.com/?p=222334)), but keeps keywords in its database until you explicitly "Save Metadata" ([Excire KB](https://support.excire.com/portal/en/kb/articles/saving-metadata-and-use-it-in-other-programs)). Peakto is Mac-only with Apple Photos sync but subscription-first (**$189 "Unlimited"** with 12 months of updates) ([Cyme press](https://cyme.io/press/topaz-photo-ai-gains-advanced-photo-management-with-peakto)), and Mylio Photos+ is **$99.99/year** only ([Mylio support](https://support.mylio.com/en/articles/8103109-how-much-does-mylio-photos-cost)), which the no-new-subscriptions rule excludes.

Apple's own stack is closing some of this gap. macOS 26 Shortcuts gained folder automations that fire on Added, Modified or Removed with a "Run Immediately" option ([Six Colors](https://sixcolors.com/post/2025/08/get-started-with-folder-automation-in-macos-tahoe/)) and a "Use Model" action that runs an Apple Intelligence model on-device or via Private Cloud Compute ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/guide/shortcuts-mac/mchl91750563/mac)); Foundation Models gained image input at WWDC 2026, with the image analysis described as on-device only ([byteiota](https://byteiota.com/apple-foundation-models-wwdc-2026-multimodal-python-sdk/)). That is a free prototype for the BUSINESS/PERSONAL fallback classifier, but nobody has published a walkthrough of folder trigger plus Use Model, its unattended behaviour is unverified, and Hazel's rule debugger and nested-folder support are worth the $42 for the production version.

For documents, Paperless-ngx is the most mature local business-versus-personal classifier. Version 3.0 (**22 July 2026**) ships native, opt-in AI with an Ollama backend that suggests title, tags, correspondent and document type; 3.1 added a workflow action that applies the suggestions automatically ([WZ-IT](https://wz-it.com/en/blog/paperless-ngx-ai-setup/)). It is configured with `PAPERLESS_AI_LLM_BACKEND=ollama` and `PAPERLESS_AI_LLM_ENDPOINT` pointing at any Ollama host, with `PAPERLESS_AI_LLM_EXTRA_PARAMS` accepting `{"reasoning_effort":"none"}` ([Paperless-ngx configuration](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/paperless-ngx/paperless-ngx/main/docs/configuration.md)). QNAP's own tutorial covers Ollama in Container Station on x86 units with at least 8 GB RAM for 3B models and 16 GB for 7B ([QNAP tutorial](https://www.qnap.com/en/how-to/tutorial/article/how-to-deploy-ollama-on-the-qnap-nas-using-container-station)); on an ARM or low-RAM QNAP, point the endpoint at Ollama on the Mac mini instead. A consume folder on the NAS, fed by Hazel or a scanner, then becomes the single intake for every receipt, invoice and letter, with storage-path rules filing business and personal documents into separate trees.

## Pick Immich or QuMagie by NAS hardware, and accept iOS limits

The NAS browsing layer depends on which QNAP it is. Immich v3.x needs **6 GB RAM minimum, 8 GB recommended**, 2–4 cores, and since v3 its machine-learning container requires the x86-64-v2 microarchitecture, with Postgres on local SSD and "never a network share" ([Immich requirements](https://docs.immich.app/install/requirements)). A TS-473A owner found QTS idling at ~9 GB with other services, upgraded to 32 GB, called 16 GB "a minimum", and switched from Container Station's app wizard to Portainer because updates would not apply ([Immich discussion #13430](https://github.com/immich-app/immich/discussions/13430)). Immich's strengths are the pieces QuMagie lacks: read-only external libraries that index the NAS date tree in place without modifying originals, CLIP natural-language search, visually-similar duplicate detection, and a `Year/Year-Month-Day` storage template for phone uploads ([Immich external libraries](https://docs.immich.app/features/libraries); [Immich storage template](https://docs.immich.app/administration/storage-template)). Its weaknesses matter for this design: moving a file inside an external library makes it a new asset and loses album membership, duplicate checking is per-library so phone uploads and the archive can double up ([Immich FAQ](https://docs.immich.app/FAQ)), face names are not written back to files (a community PR was proposed in August 2026 with unknown merge status) ([Immich discussion #15083](https://github.com/immich-app/immich/discussions/15083)), and ML acceleration supports CUDA, ROCm, OpenVINO, ARM NN and RKNN but has no Apple backend ([Immich ML acceleration](https://docs.immich.app/features/ml-hardware-acceleration)), so offloading ML to a Mac runs CPU-only. QuMagie is the zero-effort baseline on any 64-bit QNAP with QTS 4.4.1+ and 4 GB RAM ([QNAP AI Core docs](https://docs.qnap.com/application/qumagie-mobile/2.x/en-us/installing-qnap-ai-core-7DF1079.html)), but its own forum reports one person fragmented into many "who is this" groups with no way to mark false positives ([QNAP forum](https://forum.qnap.com/viewtopic.php?p=731480)) and person labels that do not survive moving files between folders ([forum.qnap.net.pl](https://forum.qnap.net.pl/threads/immich-zamiast-qumagie-ts-264-z-24-gb-ram.41962/)). PhotoPrism is lighter (3 GB RAM minimum, arm64 fine) ([PhotoPrism docs](https://docs.photoprism.app/getting-started/)) but has no first-party iOS backup and "does not yet offer the ability to write changed metadata back to the original files" ([PhotoPrism metadata docs](https://docs.photoprism.app/user-guide/library/metadata/)).

| NAS class | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| ARM, 2–4 GB (TS-x33 line) | QuMagie, or PhotoPrism; Immich only with ML disabled on the NAS and `IMMICH_MACHINE_LEARNING_URL` pointed at the Mac mini |
| x86, 8 GB | Immich works; upgrade to 16 GB if QTS runs other services |
| x86, ≥16 GB | Immich with full ML, external library on `Archive/Photos` read-write; Paperless-ngx and Ollama alongside |

Whatever runs on the NAS is a *view*, never the system of record. Keywords and person names live in the files via osxphotos and exiftool; Immich or QuMagie reads them (Immich has a "face import from EXIF/Sidecar" setting, though a user still reported the name not showing) ([Immich discussion #15076](https://github.com/immich-app/immich/discussions/15076)), and if the NAS app is replaced nothing is lost. Qfiling on the NAS sorts the landing share by file type, size and date into the destination structure with scheduled recipes ([QNAP Qfiling](https://www.qnap.com/de-de/software/qfiling)); it is the right tool for "photos to `YYYY/MM`, transcode videos", and the wrong tool for anything semantic.

Phone ingest is where expectations must be reset. QNAP's own FAQ explains that iOS decides when to launch an app's background task, gives it up to 30 seconds, and requires location access set to "Always" plus a significant position change of about 500 m ([QNAP FAQ](https://www.qnap.com/en-me/how-to/faq/con_show.php?cid=1494)); the Qfile Pro listing warns this "may noticeably decrease battery life" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id526330408)); and a TS-253D owner reported auto-upload stopped after a firmware update and "clearly does not survive a reboot of the phone" ([QNAP community](https://community.qnap.com/t/help-me-to-understand-qfile-pro/4617)). Immich is no different: "the operating system determines if a particular app can invoke background tasks" ([Immich FAQ](https://github.com/immich-app/immich/blob/main/docs/docs/FAQ.mdx)), and Apple's iOS 26.1 Background Resource Upload extension does not fit Immich's multipart API, so there is no official implementation ([Immich discussion #23245](https://github.com/immich-app/immich/discussions/23245)). PhotoSync Pro, a one-time purchase that transfers full-resolution HEIC, RAW and Live Photo pairs to SMB with folder-by-date templates ([PhotoSync](https://apps.apple.com/app/id415850124); [PhotoSync: Pro vs Premium](https://www.photosync-app.com/support/ios/answers/what-is-the-difference-between-photosync-pro-and-premium)), is the best of the bunch, but its scheduled trigger fires only "if your device is connected to the charger cable and if PhotoSync is in the list of recently used apps" ([PhotoSync support](https://www.photosync-app.com/support/ios/answers/how-do-i-automatically-transfer-photos-once-a-day)). The conclusion is that iCloud Photos should stay the capture path, with the Mac mini's "Download Originals" plus osxphotos as the guaranteed route to the NAS, and any phone-side uploader treated as an optional same-day convenience. For long video, record ProRes straight to an exFAT USB-C SSD, which iPhone 15 Pro through 17 Pro support ([Apple Support 109041](https://support.apple.com/en-kz/109041)) with a 10 Gbps cable and 220 MB/s minimum write for 4K60 ([Apple Community](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/256134492)); that footage never touches the 200 GB iCloud plan. ProRes 422 at 4K DCI 24p runs about 503 Mb/s and 422 HQ about 754 Mb/s ([ZimaSpace](https://shop.zimaspace.com/es/blogs/buying-guide/what-nas-speed-do-you-need-for-4k-video-editing)), so masters go to the NAS and proxies are generated locally for editing.

## Roll out in five phases over three months

| Phase | When | Work | One-time cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Freeze and protect | Week 1 | Enable daily QNAP snapshots; Time Machine from every Mac to the QNAP; clone the old photo trees to a cold SSD before touching them | $0 (or $49.99 ChronoSync/CCC) |
| 1. Build the hub | Weeks 1–2 | Mac mini: auto-login, UPS, Photos library on APFS SSD, "Download Originals" on, "Optimize Mac Storage" off; install Hazel 6.1, TrueSync (one offline inbox folder), Drive for desktop (Mirror for `Dump`); create `iCloud Drive/Inbox`; Hazel rules for `biz`/`home` tags and name patterns | $42 Hazel |
| 2. Migrate the photo archive | Weeks 2–4 | czkawka exact-duplicate pass; exiftool folder-path → `XMP:Subject`; re-file by `DateTimeOriginal` into `Archive/Photos/YYYY/YYYY-MM-DD_slug`; finish naming people in Apple Photos; nightly osxphotos `--update --person-keyword --exiftool` launchd job to the NAS | $14.99 PhotoSweeper (optional) |
| 3. Add the NAS layers | Month 2 | Immich (x86, ≥16 GB) or QuMagie as read-only view; Qfiling recipe on `Inbox/`; HBS 3 ≥26.2.0.938 encrypted, versioned job `Archive/` → Google Drive; Paperless-ngx consume folder | $0 (RAM upgrade if needed) |
| 4. Add local AI | Month 3+ | Ollama with `qwen3-vl:8b` on the mini or Studio; exif-ai batch over the archive with a fixed keyword list and `--dry-run` first; Paperless AI suggestions pointed at the same Ollama; optional Shortcuts "Use Model" fallback for BUSINESS/PERSONAL | $0 |

Optional extras are PhotoSync Pro for same-day phone copies (one-time, listed at roughly $6–25 by aggregators ([AlternativeTo](https://www.alternativeto.net/software/photosync/about/))) and Excire Foto 2027 at $249 only if a GUI cataloguer is wanted instead of digiKam. Everything else in the design is free: osxphotos, ExifTool, czkawka, Ollama, mlx-vlm, exif-ai, Immich, Paperless-ngx, Qfiling, HBS 3, QuMagie and QNAP snapshots.

The list of things to stop doing is as important as the tool list. Stop creating per-family and per-event photo folders; the date tree plus keywords replaces them and the exiftool pass converts the old ones. Stop hand-sorting phone photos; osxphotos does it nightly from the names already in Apple Photos. Stop making project folders for raw family clips; only edited films get the 01–05 template. Stop keeping any Photos library or FCP library on the NAS. Stop letting more than one service claim Desktop & Documents. Stop expecting QNAP's or Immich's iOS app to upload overnight on its own. Do not point HBS 3 or a continuous rclone job at Zoho, do not run rclone against iCloud with Advanced Data Protection on, and do not treat Finder tags as something another device will see. Do not enable Drive for desktop's Photos Library upload on the mini, which would push iCloud media into Google Photos and eat the shared Google quota ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2021/10/22/google-drive-desktop-mac-m1-support/)).

The remaining manual budget, drawn from practitioner routines, is about 15 minutes a week emptying one inbox and a smart album of "last 7 days, no keyword" ([Jamie Rubin](https://jamierubin.net/2021/07/09/the-weekly-playbook-2-curating-photos/)), 30 minutes a month checking that HBS 3, Time Machine and the osxphotos job succeeded, reviewing new keywords against the controlled list and rotating the cold SSD, and about two hours a year to cull one old year, test-restore one folder from Google Drive and one from a snapshot, and archive finished video projects.

| Limitation | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| iOS background upload | Every PhotoKit uploader (Qfile, QuMagie, Immich, PhotoSync) is opportunistic; the Mac mini plus iCloud is the guaranteed path |
| Zoho WorkDrive has no NAS bridge | No WebDAV, no HBS 3 provider, rclone throttled; only TrueSync on the mini moves files in, and Time Machine of the offline folder is the only owned backup |
| Apple face data is export-only | Names leave as keywords via osxphotos; rectangles and clusters stay in Photos; later naming in Photos propagates only on the next export, never back from the NAS |
| VLMs cannot identify people | Scene and object keywords only; family-branch mapping is a lookup table from recognised names |
| Immich metadata fragility | Moving files loses Immich-side albums; faces not written to XMP; uploads and external library do not dedupe against each other; CPU-only on Macs |
| QuMagie and PhotoPrism | QuMagie loses labels on file moves and has no documented export; PhotoPrism writes nothing back to files |
| Headless Mac | Hazel and sync clients stop at the login screen after a power cut; needs auto-login and a UPS |
| 200 GB iCloud+ and Mac SSD space | The mini must have attached SSD capacity for the full Photos library; iCloud stays a capture buffer, not the archive |
| HBS 3 encryption | The password can never be changed; losing it loses the off-site copy |
| Unverified | Whether Shortcuts "Use Model" accepts images or runs unattended; which Google edition the "Pro" account is; exact Qfile Pro folder output; osxphotos on macOS 26 is lightly tested |

## Conclusion

The shift this report argues for is from *organising* to *describing*: once a photo carries "Jane Smith", "Smith side" and its capture date inside the file, every view the family wants (by branch, by birthday, by year, by who was there) is a query in Photos, Immich or Spotlight rather than a folder someone has to create and keep consistent. The folder tree becomes a machine-generated storage address, which is why an 18-year-old archival convention (date-first names) outlasts every clever taxonomy and why the migration can be done with one exiftool command rather than a weekend of dragging.

The second-order consequence is that the Mac mini, not the NAS, is the control plane. The NAS stores and snapshots; the clouds sync and share; but only a logged-in Mac can see all three clouds, run Apple's face recognition, and host Hazel. Treat the mini as infrastructure (UPS, auto-login, a monthly glance at its logs) and the rest of the system needs almost no attention. The parts still worth watching over the next year are Immich's face write-back pull request, Apple's unattended "Use Model" behaviour in Shortcuts, and any sign of a WebDAV or NAS connector from Zoho; any one of those landing would simplify the design further, but none is needed for it to work today.
