## 2. Strongest arguments (ranked)

### A. The hearing notice was only **7 days**, not 14 — likely a fatal procedural defect
- Hearing letter is dated **07/14/2026**; hearing was **07/21/2026** (7 days).
- **Bylaws §8.1(A):** hearing "after reasonable notice of **not less than fourteen (14) days**."
- **Association's own Rules (preamble, Rev. 10/21/25):** "Prior to the imposition of any fine, homeowners will receive **14 days' written notice** and the opportunity for a hearing before an independent Hearing Committee, in compliance with Florida statute."
- **Fla. Stat. §720.305(2)** requires at least 14 days' notice before a fine can be imposed **[VERIFY text]**.
- It was sent by certified mail on 7/14, so you actually *received* it later than 7/14 – the real gap is smaller still. The "Receipt #" line on the letter is blank; pull USPS tracking/your green card for the delivery date.
- Callahan's email never addresses this. This is your best leverage: a fine imposed without the required notice is not validly imposed.

### B. The notice itself was legally thin
Bylaws §8.1(A) requires the notice to include (2) the specific provision alleged violated, (3) **"a short and plain statement of the specific facts"** and (4) the possible fine. The letter says only "Trash cans need to be concealed" – no date observed, no photo, no address-specific facts, and it cites only Rule 7. Callahan now relies on **Rule 14(f)** and **Rule 3(e)/7(a) (no ARC application)** – neither was cited in the hearing letter. Rule 7(a)'s "ARC application required" language is quoted in the letter, but the *fact* alleged was only visibility. The no-application issue was raised **for the first time on Oct 8**, so it was never noticed or heard and **cannot support the $1,000 fine already imposed**.

### C. Rule 7(b) builds in a **12-month growth period** for plant screening
Rule 7(b): plants "must be of a variety that will grow to the required height and width to conceal the unit(s) **within a twelve (12) month period**." Newly installed plants (installed ~Jun 10) are *supposed* to still be growing in. "Still visible on Jul 21" – six weeks after install – isn't evidence the method fails; the Rule contemplates it. The only real question is whether the *variety* will conceal within 12 months. That is why the **plant name matters** – if it's on the HOA's recommended list (§6), you've met the Rule's own test.

### D. The fine accrual/dates don't add up
- The hearing letter says fines "become effective immediately upon the Committee rendering its verdict" (i.e., Jul 21) and are **$100/day, max $1,000**. Reaching $1,000 would take 10 days *after* the verdict.
- Callahan says the full $1,000 was assessed because the violation "remained uncorrected through the **10-day correction period provided in the notice**." **The hearing letter contains no 10-day correction period.** Ask for the 1st/2nd notices – if none state it, that justification is invented after the fact.
- Ask: when did the verdict get **communicated to you in writing**? If you were never told of the verdict, daily fines can't reasonably run against you.

### E. Evidence problem (your observation)
Callahan again relies on photos from the June notice, which you say pre-date the plants. The committee supposedly judged "conditions as they existed on [Jul 21]." Demand the **actual evidence packet**: photos with date stamps from on/after the install, who took them, and the committee minutes. If their only photos pre-date Jun 10, the committee decided on stale evidence – and your Jun 13–15 photos plus the Sept 19 photos are *better* evidence of the actual condition.
- ⚠️ **One thing I noticed in your own evidence – fix before you rely on it:** your Oct 5 email says IMG_1403 "was created on June 10th." The Info-window screenshot you attached shows **Modified: Saturday, June 13, 2026, 8:14 PM** (the "Created June 15" is just the date the file was copied into Zoho). It does **not** show June 10. Pull the true capture date (EXIF/Photos app "Date Taken") and send a corrected screenshot, or the manager may say your claim is unsupported. Your receipts (Jun 9–10) help corroborate install timing.

### F. Committee composition / independence
Bylaws §8.1(B): panel of **three owners appointed by the Board, none a Director/Officer/employee or their relative**; majority must agree. Callahan calls it the "Fine Review Committee." Ask for: names/eligibility, how many voted, minutes (Bylaws §3.10 – minutes must be available to owners). Any irregularity here also voids the fine.

### G. Uniform enforcement
Bylaws §7: rules must be "**uniformly applied and enforced**." Photograph other homes in the community with visible bins/unscreened equipment (particularly any not fined). Selective enforcement strengthens a hardship/waiver request.

### H. Hardship / equitable mitigation (use, but it's not a legal exemption)
Birth, complications, NICU stay – documented by hospital discharge/birth records. Not a defense to the rule, but the Board has discretion: the Rules say fines "**may**" be assessed "up to" $100/day/$1,000 and Bylaws §8.1 says fines are "in amounts deemed necessary… to deter future violations." The violation is **now cured/being cured** – deterrence is already accomplished. Callahan's own email is sympathetic ("congratulations… I understand").

## 3. Weak spots / risks (be ready for these)

1. **No ARC application was filed.** Rule 3(a), 7(a), 17(a): any landscaping change and any concealment method needs ARC *and* Board approval **before** installing. The ARC form itself (Term 6) warns unapproved completed work "may be subject to a daily fine up to $1,000 and removal." **Rule 3(e)** lets the Board require removal. A *second* violation is possible and Callahan has previewed it.
   - Mitigation: you did it to *cure* a violation (they told you to conceal), within the 12-month-growth framework, using the *recommended* method. Don't claim you filed anything unless you did.
2. **"Paved area" / potted plants.** Callahan describes the installation as "in the paved area." **Rule 17(e): potted plants must be in landscape beds, not on the driveway/sidewalk.** If your plants are in pots on pavement, that's a separate exposure – and may explain why they call it "insufficient." Consider moving them into a bed or planting in-ground.
3. **You didn't report the cure.** The 2026 procedure memo (Troy Lyvers) says to email name, address, rule cited, and a photo showing correction. The first photos went to Callahan Oct 5 – after the fine. Emphasize that you were hospital-bound/NICU, not ignoring.
4. **Fine = $1,000 exactly.** Under Fla. Stat. §720.305/§720.3085 a fine under $1,000 can't become a lien, but a $1,000 fine can **[VERIFY]**. Don't ignore it. Also note the Bylaws §8.1 sentence "continuing violation shall **not** be limited to $1,000.00" is evidently a drafting error – but it is a reminder that a bad-faith reading could be argued; don't rely on it.
5. **Separate master-association delinquency.** `IMG_1527` (The Forum at Fort Myers Association, 6/12/2026) shows **$913.94 past due** (assessment $562.66 + $250 late fee + $101.28 interest). That's a different association than Promenade, but (a) the ARC form says applicants "must be in good standing" and (b) it weakens a goodwill plea. If it's still open, pay it before you submit anything.
6. **Statute-vs-bylaws on per-day fines.** The Association's Rules set $100/day up to $1,000. Statute §720.305(2) generally permits a per-day fine up to $1,000 aggregate for continuing violations after one notice/hearing **[VERIFY]**, so don't count on a cap argument alone.

## 4. Statute notes (secondary sources only – verify)
- §720.305(2): fines require **14 days' written notice** and hearing before a committee of ≥3 non-board, non-relative members; the committee must **confirm** the fine by majority; the Board then imposes it. (Confirmed by multiple secondary summaries.)
- A 2024 change (HB 1203) reportedly bars a fine if the violation is cured before the hearing – **I could not confirm this**; if true, it would matter only if the plants were deemed compliant on Jul 21.
- §720.3035: ARC decisions must be in writing, ideally citing the provision for denial; the Rules (preamble and 3(c)) say the ARC must act within **30 days** or the application is "considered approved by Florida statute," and **denials must be in writing and cite the specific governing document provision**. Ask for the ARC's **written** determination – Callahan says the ARC "reviewed the area" and found it inadequate, but no application existed. What did they review, and where is the written denial with the cited provision?
